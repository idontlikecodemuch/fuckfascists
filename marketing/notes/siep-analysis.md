## The answer to your concern

**Authorization does not take a side, and it does not label the app.** It is an identity check plus a disclosure. Meta checks the ID of the person running the ads. Each declared ad then shows "Paid for by App Hold Media LLC", and the creative, a spend range, an impression range and demographics go into the public Ad Library for 7 years. Meta's own description: these ads "can come from a range of advertisers. They include activists, brands, non-profit groups and political organizations" ([About social issues](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/214754279118974)) [V]. Strictly nonpartisan groups sit in the same archive under their own names: Ballotpedia, League of Women Voters, Vote.org. So do a for-profit "no spin" news app (Ground News, thousands of ads running this week), NBCUniversal, and Meta itself ("Paid for by Facebook, Inc."). All verified below. The disclaimer shows who paid, not which side the ad is on. Your ID is never shown publicly [V].

**What decides whether FCK's ads are "about politics" is the ad content, not the authorization.** Meta says so: "Our policy … is focused on the content of the ad" ([About SIEP ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/167836590566506)) [V]. FCK's ads would normally carry several of Meta's named triggers:
- content "about … a political party [or] a political action committee": corporate PAC totals shown as R:/D:
- "political ideologies": the word fascists
- a political figure's image: Musk, on the scorecard
- a landing page that says "Track political funding"

If you don't authorize, the ads don't become non-political. When they are caught, they are "paused, disapproved and added to the Ad Library," and the label reads **"This ad ran without a required disclaimer."** That is a 7-year public record that looks like dodging the rules. It has happened to ProPublica, the New York Times and Netflix. So the real choice is not "political app or not." It is one of three things:
- Our company name on a disclosure.
- An evasion label.
- No paid Meta ads at all.

Authorization does cost something real: App Hold Media LLC becomes, in Meta's data, an advertiser in the political/issue category. Researchers and journalists who pull the Ad Library Report will see it there. Only option D avoids that.

## 1. What the policy says (operative text)

- **Definition** [V] ([167836590566506](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/167836590566506)): an ad is SIEP when it is "Made by, on behalf of, or about a candidate for public office, a political figure, a political party, a political action committee or advocates for the outcome of an election…; or About any election…; or About social issues in any place where the ad is being placed; or Regulated as political advertising." Same in the [policy summary](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/SIEP-advertising/SIEP/) [V].
- **US social issues** [V] ([214754279118974](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/214754279118974)): Civil and social rights · Crime · Economy · Education · Environmental politics · Guns · Health · Immigration · Political values and governance · Security and foreign policy.
- **Political values and governance** [V] ([313752069181919](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/313752069181919)): "discussion, debate or advocacy for or against topics including … **political ideologies**, institutional governance." Meta's own examples:
  - Requires a disclaimer: "We need campaign finance reform to level the playing field for candidates!"
  - Does not: "Want to understand more how government works? Join our seminar."
  - The same page lists as triggers "the name of a political figure … or their image." Political figures "include officials appointed by politicians."
- **Product carve-out** [V] ([287622936276216](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/287622936276216)): no disclaimer if "A product or service is prominently shown, shown in use or named … AND The primary purpose of the ad is to sell a product or promote a service, even if the ad content includes advocacy for a social issue AND … a call-to-action." "Install now" and "Use app" count as CTAs. **Exclusions that matter here:**
  - "This doesn't apply to products or services about or with mentions of politicians, political parties, elections or legislation."
  - It still requires a disclaimer if the ad "contains social issue advocacy messaging on the product itself … or in the name of the service itself."
  - "Brand-focused messaging without a clear call-to-action … may still require" one.
  - Meta examples: an Earth-Day hemp T-shirt ad passes. An embroidered "Support refugees" patch needs a disclaimer. So does a documentary *titled* as advocacy ("Stream now" doesn't save it).
- **Review** [V]: the carve-out page says "Ad review can take up to 72 hours… our enforcement isn't perfect." The [Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/) say "Ads remain subject to review and re-review at all times, and may be rejected or restricted… at any time." On violation: "the ad will be rejected, and the Business Account or its assets may be restricted." Appeals go through Request review / Account Quality.

## 2. Authorization is disclosure, not an endorsement — Ad Library evidence

**What it is** [V] ([208949576550051](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/208949576550051), [creating-disclaimers](https://www.facebook.com/government-nonprofits/blog/creating-disclaimers), [198009284345835](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/198009284345835)):
- The founder's personal profile verifies a government ID and turns on 2FA. The ID "won't be visible on your profile, in ads or to other admins."
- The Page then gets a disclaimer naming the payer. Meta offers five ways to back it:
  - EIN, FEC ID, or .gov: ads show a "Confirmed Organization" icon.
  - Contact details (website, email, phone, address), or Page name: ads show an "About this ad" icon.
- Disclaimers are reviewed "within 24 hours." They must not contain "profanity, objectionable language" or URLs, so the disclaimer has to read "App Hold Media LLC", not "FCK Fascists."
- Declaring is per ad or per campaign (the Special Ad Category). Being authorized does not force every ad into the category.

## 3. What the label touches

- **On the ad** [V]: "Sponsored · Paid for by App Hold Media LLC" under the Page name, plus the Confirmed-Organization or About-this-ad icon. The disclaimer stays when the ad is shared. Every ad already has "Why am I seeing this ad."
- **In the Ad Library, public, logged out, 7 years** [V] ([2405092116183307](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2405092116183307)):
  - the creative and run dates
  - spend and impression *ranges*
  - age, gender and location split
  - estimated audience size
  - the disclaimer's contact info (phone, email, website; the street address can be masked)
  - advertiser targeting choices, aggregated per Page
  - lifetime and last-week SIEP spend on the Page's transparency panel
  - inclusion in the downloadable Ad Library Report

  Ads "retroactively deemed" SIEP are archived the same way, with no disclaimer.
- **Who sees the ads**: people who switched off SIEP ads in Ad Preferences or Ad Topics won't see declared ads (Meta's [2026 US elections brief](https://www.meta.com/sr/meta-2026-us-elections/), PDF) [V]. That is a modest reach cut. SIEP ads can't run on Messenger, Audience Network, Marketplace, Search or the right-hand column [V]. Our Reels/Stories plan is unaffected.
- **Page ownership**: US Pages running SIEP ads must have a Page owner. A disclaimer can serve as the owner, so the Page's transparency panel shows the LLC ([Meta help, mirrored](https://www.messenger.com/help/messenger-app/537496786854776)) [S].
- **Organic reach and the "political content" control**: Instagram classifies *each post* by content ("likely to mention governments, elections, or social topics…") ([The Markup, 2024](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/03/25/instagram-creators-check-if-your-posts-are-political)) [S]. In Jan 2025 Meta moved to a "more personalized" approach and the control's default to "standard" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/07/meta-to-phase-back-in-political-content-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads), [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/threads-and-instagram-will-recommend-political-content-now-says-adam-mosseri-460084-2025-01-09)) [S]. I found no Meta document linking ad authorization to organic distribution [I]. Organic exposure depends on what we *post*, and that is the same with or without authorization.
- **App Store / Google Play**: no effect [I, high confidence]. Store category and age rating (Reference, 16+) are set by us and by Apple/Google review against their own guidelines. No data flows from Meta's ad system to the stores, and no store guideline refers to an ad platform's classification.
- **Permanence**: authorization is reversible. Turning off 2FA revokes it, Page admins can remove disclaimers, and declaring is chosen per ad [V]. **The archive is not reversible**: every declared ad stays public for 7 years.

## 4. Does the brand itself trigger SIEP?

On Meta's text, very likely yes [I, strong]:
1. **Political ideology.** "Political values and governance" names "political ideologies." "FCK FASCISTS" is advocacy against one. The word sits in the advocacy part of the archive: about 5,700 SIEP ads contain "fascists" (e.g. PowerSwitch Action 1294312949435983, Oct 7, 2026: "Stop letting fascists use Amazon's data centers") [V].
2. **The sponsor line is the Page name.** Every ad, however product-only, shows "FCK Fascists · Sponsored." That is advocacy "in the name of the service itself," which is excluded from the carve-out.
3. **Party and PAC data is the product.** R:/D: and "Both parties" are "mentions of political parties." The carve-out explicitly doesn't apply to those. Corporate PAC totals are "about … a political action committee." Snap uses the same wording: ads about PACs are election-related ([Snap](https://snap.com/en-US/ad-policies/political)) [V].
4. **The scorecard asset fails on its face.** I read the Jul 11 card. It shows the FCK FASCISTS logo, "The fascists won't FCK themselves." (advocacy on the product output), FCKFASCISTS.COM, and Musk's likeness (a 2025 appointed official, so plausibly a "political figure"; Meta doesn't publish its list).
5. **The destination is reviewed too.** The App Store page says "Track political funding" and "political contributions" [V].

**Could a stripped ad pass undeclared?** It would need App Store name only, no R/D, no PAC, no "political," no CEO faces, no logo, and an "Install now" CTA. Then it can't show what the app does, and points 2 and 5 still apply. My estimate: it might clear first review, but with real odds of rejection or a later flag ("re-review at all times") [I].

**Enforcement precedent:**
- Road 96, an indie *game*, was rejected in July 2021 for "escape from a country in turmoil" and "reach the border." Meta offered two options: edit the ad or get authorized ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/facebook-blocks-i-road-96-i-ad-for-being-political-messaging-)) [S].
- Bush's Beans and others were pulled in 2018 over the word "Bush" ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/social-media/bush-s-beans-reverend-named-clinton-facebook-s-political-ad-n888561)) [S].
- Neither a game frame nor an innocent brand name protected the ad.

**Account consequence:** an undeclared SIEP ad gets "paused, disapproved and added to the Ad Library until the advertiser completes the authorization process" [V]. Big brands absorb a few of these (Netflix, NYT). For a *new, small* ad account, "too many rejected ads" leading to restriction is widely reported but not quantified by Meta [S/unverified]. Resubmitting tweaked versions of rejected ads is a known trigger for "circumventing systems" enforcement [S].

**A separate risk that applies to every option** ([Profanity](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/profanity/)) [V]: "Profanity and obscene language, even when it's obscure, misspelled or vague," including via "symbols." The F★CK star-for-U in the logo and "I FCK'D" are exposed to this. The acronym exception covers only acronyms that "stand for profane language" without spelling it, so it's a judgement call for "FCK, Financial Contribution Kit" [I].

## 5. The options, weighed

**Midterm freeze** [S, consistent across sources]: new SIEP ads are blocked from **12:01 AM PT Oct 27 to 11:59 PM PT Nov 3, 2026** (Public Technologies brief, Sep 14, via [Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15577179852325888)).
- Meta's own brief confirms the "final week" block, and that ads "that have previously run" continue [V].
- Secondary sources add two rules: an ad needs **≥1 impression before the cutoff**, and during the freeze only bid, budget, schedule and pause can change [S].
- In 2024 Meta [extended the block past Election Day](https://www.axios.com/2024/11/04/meta-election-political-ads-ban-extension) [S].
- So under A or C, ads must be *live* by about Oct 23–24, since impressions can take 6–24h to register.

## 6. "Educational, not political" — does Meta draw that line?

- **Partly, and only on the social-issue side.** Neutral education is outside the category: "Want to understand more how government works? Join our seminar." So is a museum exhibit on civil rights. But "discussion" is covered, not just advocacy. And the elections/politics prong (parties, PACs, political figures) has **no informational exception** [V].
- **News publisher exemption** [V] ([387111852028957](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/387111852028957)): only "News publishers registered in the news Page index," with ads whose "primary purpose [is] reporting on news." Opinion still needs a disclaimer.
- The index needs a 90-day-old Page, original reporting, and "a multi-person editorial staff" ([guidelines](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/270254993785210)) [S]. A one-person app selling downloads doesn't qualify, and we shouldn't claim to be news.
- **What the precedent does show:** an educational, nonpartisan identity is compatible with declaring. Ballotpedia, LWV, Kite & Key (education), Ground News ("think for yourself") and Goods Unite Us ("UNBIASED and NONPARTISAN") all carry "Paid for by." Declaring is how those brands paid for reach; it is not where they took sides.

## 7. Recommendation: **C, with a hard date, and D as the honest fallback**

1. **Authorize now** under the disclaimer **"App Hold Media LLC."** Try the EIN route for the "Confirmed Organization" icon; whether an LLC's EIN paperwork is accepted is unverified. Classification is close to certain given the Page name, the data and the landing page, so declaring is the only version where the public record reads as a company disclosing rather than one evading.
2. **Keep the creative product-first and party-symmetric.** Show the app working: map tap, barcode, card. Put R and D side by side, always both. Use "Install now." Inside a declared ad, "Both parties. On file." is fine. It would sink a carve-out attempt, but we aren't attempting one.
3. **Don't run B as a probe.** The downside is lopsided: a 7-year evasion label and strikes on a brand-new account, in exchange for an ad that can't show the product.
4. **Rework the scorecard still before paid use** (whatever the option): drop the tagline "The fascists won't FCK themselves," and avoid Musk on paid cards. Expect profanity review on the F★CK logo; test one ad first.
5. **Hard date:** if authorization plus a delivered impression isn't done by **Oct 23**, hold all Meta paid until Nov 4. Don't fill the freeze week with undeclared ads, the week reviewers are most alert [I].
6. If you weigh "listed as a political/issue advertiser for 7 years" above reach, **D is legitimate.** OpenSecrets, Buycott, Boycat and Progressive Shopper show no SIEP history. Pair it with party-free Google app ads.

## What we'd never do (in ads, either option)
- No party words as claims: no "Republican/Democrat" verbs, no "stop the GOP." R: and D: appear only as labels on records, side by side.
- No "boycott," no "dark money," no "fascist" pinned on a named person or company, no "vote" or "election" calls to action.
- No candidates, officeholders, or political appointees in creative. No CEO faces in paid ads.
- No "nonpartisan news" or "news publisher" claims; we are a game and a reference tool.
- No resubmitting a rejected ad in disguise. A rejection is a decision point, not a puzzle.
- No disclaimer other than the legal entity, and never the brand slogan.
- No boosting from the Instagram app, and no undeclared ads during Oct 27–Nov 3.
